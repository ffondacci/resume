Envision binoculars

2025 - Current

Unistellar

AR smart binoculars, iOS & Android

  • Context: Binoculars that overlay augmented reality (peak names, terrain silhouettes, sky objects) on what the user sees. Built the whole app side alone on iOS, then ported it to Android: connection, control, data transfer, firmware updates, calibration.
  • Bluetooth protocol: Custom binary TLV over BLE GATT (sync header, command, length, payload, CRC), byte-identical on iOS and Android. MTU negotiated to ~500 bytes (Android defaults to 23). Silent auto-connect, one-device guard, recovery from out-of-range, power-off and background.
  • Firmware over the air: Multi-chip updates over Bluetooth: dual-core STM32, nRF radio and on-device file system. The app diffs the cloud manifest against the one on the device, downloads only what changed, checks every file by SHA-256, streams packages into a free A/B slot, verifies versions after reboot and rewrites the device manifest. Rescue mode for bricked units, remote kill switch and staged rollout without an app release.
  • Terrain AR: Cancellable 6-step pipeline: precise GPS fix, OpenStreetMap terrain silhouettes and points of interest computed on the phone, device buffers flushed, bulk write to the binoculars with live progress and ETA. Follow mode re-syncs when the user moves.
  • Calibration 360: Guided flow that teaches the binoculars a map of their own compass errors all around the user, with magnetic-interference detection.

App architecture

2025 - Current

Unistellar

Designed for the rebuild: modular, shared, astronomy at the core

  • Modularity: iOS app split into ~40 feature modules (catalog, observation, science, gallery, connection, firmware, binoculars…) on top of 14 Swift packages with a strict, layered dependency graph: common → models, logging, Bluetooth → database, astronomy, binocular protocol → catalog. Android mirrors the same modules and names, so features port module by module.
  • Layering: Services talk to the hardware (transport, retries, fallback), stores own state and decide when to act, views only display. One responsibility per type, each layer replaceable by a mock for tests and debug devices.
  • Shared code: C++ planetarium engine compiled for both platforms, Swift packages consumed through SPM, Android shared library consumed as a Git submodule with its own sample app.
  • Astronomy engine: Julian date, local apparent sidereal time, precession, RA/Dec ↔ alt/az; trajectories of moving objects interpolated from ephemerides; root-finding for rise, set and observable windows; visibility of the whole catalog computed in batches for the user's place and night.

Unistellar app

Apr 2025 - Current

Unistellar

Full rebuild of the iOS & Android telescope app

  • Context: Complete rebuild of the native companion app of the Unistellar telescopes, on iOS and Android, replacing the previous generation: new architecture, SwiftUI and Jetpack Compose, telescopes driven over Wi-Fi. Discovery and connection, live control and observation, science campaigns, catalog of celestial objects, firmware updates.
  • Concurrency: Swift Concurrency with actor-isolated services and Observation-based stores; Kotlin coroutines and Flow on Android. One owner per piece of state, Sendable types across boundaries, cancellation propagated end to end and continuations resumed exactly once.
  • Performance: Telescope events arrive several times per second: throttled and published only on change. Device overheating investigated with Instruments through A/B runs (renderer on/off, terrain on/off), cause isolated in the planetarium below.
  • Delivery: Store releases on both platforms with Fastlane and GitLab CI, firmware and object catalog embedded in each build, native symbols uploaded to Crashlytics.
  • Stack: Swift, SwiftUI, Swift Concurrency, Swift Packages, Core Bluetooth. Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, coroutines & Flow, Koin, Room. Firebase (Auth, Firestore, Functions, Crashlytics, Messaging).

Planetarium

Sep 2025 - Current

Unistellar

Cross-platform C++ planetarium engine on bgfx

  • Context: Real-time sky at the heart of the app: stars, planets, deep-sky objects, terrain and horizon, shared by iOS and Android. Engine created by a teammate; I followed it from September 2025 and carried most of its new features on both platforms.
  • Rendering: Vulkan and OpenGL ES back-ends on Android next to Metal on iOS, validated on device. Coordinate grids, horizon ring with cardinal points, star trails, ecliptic, night vision, procedural Milky Way, aurora and airglow, procedural planet shader, solar eclipse with corona and animated prominences.
  • Animation & camera: Intro that opens the sky 24 h ahead and glides back to now, sunrise and sunset intro with eased twilight, smooth time-scrub camera, follow-selection camera, crossfades so no object ever pops.
  • Two platforms: Every engine feature exposed through native bindings and shipped on iOS and Android together. Native calls serialized behind a single lock on Android, ABI checks on the exported Android library, labels in Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and Japanese without overlap.
  • Performance: Frame loop profiled at 60 fps on phones: per-frame allocations, cache rebuilds and GPU buffer uploads isolated as the source of overheating; thermal state tracked as a first-class metric.
